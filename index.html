KEY INSIGHT

The 3D earthquake distribution reveals more than earthquake locations. It provides a visual representation of the underlying tectonic structure and the descending Pacific Plate beneath Japan.

Earthquake Distribution

Most earthquakes around Japan occur offshore, particularly along the Pacific coast. This distribution reflects tectonic plate interactions and major subduction zones.

Plate Subduction

The Pacific Plate moves westward and subducts beneath the tectonic plates surrounding Japan. As the plate descends into the Earth's mantle, it generates earthquakes at progressively greater depths.

Wadati–Benioff Zone

The deep earthquake clusters visible in this 3D visualization form a descending seismic zone known as the Wadati–Benioff Zone. This pattern reveals the approximate geometry of the subducting tectonic plate.

Deep Earthquake Activity

Earthquakes occurring hundreds of kilometres below the surface are associated with the descending plate. Their distribution provides insight into the depth, angle and structure of the subduction process.

Will Japan Sink?

Japan will not simply sink into the ocean. Tectonic activity causes gradual deformation and local uplift or subsidence, while Japan sits on the overriding plate. Its geography will evolve over millions of years through tectonic movement, volcanic activity, erosion and sea-level changes.
